# 4. Analyse

## Zapier — product roundup and practical benchmark

- **Angle / format:** current small-team software roundup with use-case recommendations and a comparison table.
- **Strengths:** names selection criteria, explains product fit, and gives readers a direct choice path. Quote: “there's no real one-size-fits-all solution.”
- **Weaknesses:** its recommendations depend on hands-on testing that this example cannot reproduce. Quote: “I've been testing these apps for a number of years.”
- **Gap:** it is useful for product categories, but a vendor-neutral FAQ can explain how to define requirements before looking at products.
- **Claim carried forward:** integrations and permissions are practical selection criteria. Quote: “Integration possibilities.”

Read: 2026-11-02. URL: https://zapier.com/blog/best-project-management-software-small-business/

## Small Business Administration — adoption and risk checklist

- **Angle / format:** general small-business software checklist, written for owners rather than project-management specialists.
- **Strengths:** warns against complexity, recommends integrations, growth planning, trials, and low-risk terms. Quote: “Not necessarily. If you have a small team, getting more features and functionality can leave you worse off.”
- **Weaknesses:** it is not specific to project workflows, task dependencies, or project views.
- **Gap:** translate the general advice into questions a small team can ask about its actual work.
- **Claim carried forward:** a trial or month-to-month option reduces lock-in. Quote: “Free trials enable you to do a hands-on evaluation for a week or two.”

Read: 2026-11-02. URL: https://www.sba.gov/blog/2018/2018-06/checklist-choosing-business-software/

## Project Management Institute — requirements-first selection method

- **Angle / format:** expert guidance on evaluating project-management software by business requirements and system fit.
- **Strengths:** puts business needs before features and calls out interface, integration, support, scalability, and data exchange. Quote: “focus on business requirements.”
- **Weaknesses:** the article is older and uses examples from a different software era; product-specific advice is intentionally limited.
- **Gap:** make the requirements exercise concrete for a small business and connect it to a short FAQ.
- **Claim carried forward:** the tool should support the project, not dictate it. Quote: “software supports the project and not vice versa.”

Read: 2026-11-02. URL: https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/guideline-evaluate-project-management-software-4629

## Synthesis

All three pages support a requirements-first article, but none provides a compact FAQ that walks a small-business reader from workflow mapping through a low-risk trial and exit check. That is the differentiator. The article will not name a universal winner or claim personal testing.
